02 / THE FRAMEWORK

Anatomy meets
multimodal learning.

MARGIN combines patient-specific resection-plane information with CT representations across spatial scales to estimate microscopic margin status.

  1. 01

    Segment & reconstruct

    CT → biliary and tumor masks → reviewed 3D biliary anatomy.

  2. 02

    Represent across scales

    Spatial anatomy + local 3D Mamba features + global Liver-DINO features.

  3. 03

    Fuse & predict

    Multimodal feature fusion for patient-level R0 / R1 prediction.

03 / RESEARCH FINDINGS

Evaluated across
three clinical centers.

A retrospective multicenter study of patients undergoing curative-intent resection for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma, with an independent external test cohort.

410Patients in the study
176External test patients
0.87External-test AUROC

AUROC and accuracy above measure MARGIN margin-status prediction, not nnU-Net segmentation performance.

SUPPORTING SURGEON ASSESSMENT

A quantitative
second reader.

In a paired study with five hepatobiliary surgeons, MARGIN assistance improved mean reader accuracy and sensitivity while maintaining high mean specificity.

Mean accuracy0.71 → 0.84
Mean sensitivity0.20 → 0.62

Unassisted → MARGIN-assisted assessment

Research context

These findings support further evaluation of MARGIN as a decision-support framework. The study was retrospective and restricted to resected patients within one country. Prospective validation, including comparison of alternative surgical plans for an individual patient, remains necessary.
